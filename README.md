## Overview
In applications such as genealogy and health, there exist many datasets that contain potentially overlapping information. This presents the opportunity and challenge of record linkage: determining which pieces of information refer to the same individual. Because of the vast number of pairs of records that could possibly be considered, human experts can only take care of a small portion of linkage decisions. Record linkage algorithms have thus been devised to automate the process. Under the tutelage of Dr. Dennis Tolley, I investigated a naive Bayes record linkage algorithm with data simulated in R. I used Gemini to aid in statistical and coding tasks, and I invested much time and thought into solving the problem and checking the AI output. 

## Presentation
This PowerPoint file introduces record linkage and the algorithm we were concerned with (it is called the "White model" in the slides). I discuss how we evaluated the algorithm's performance and a few important conclusions. 

## Report
This paper builds on the presentation by evaluating the algorithm's performance when its statistical assumptions are broken in a particular context.
